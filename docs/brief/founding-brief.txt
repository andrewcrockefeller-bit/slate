HOW TO READ THIS
----------------
This is the document the project instructions refer to and did not yet contain.
It defines four things the instructions assume exist: what v1 is and is not, the
four-level hint ladder, the tutor loop's trigger events, and the milestone
sequence. It does not restate the five invariants — those live in the project
instructions and govern everything below.

Where I made a call you may disagree with, the call is marked OPINION with the
reasoning attached. Those are the parts to argue with. Everything else is
mechanical.

Companion document: brief/origin-context.txt (the prototype origin story and
problem statement). This brief is the answer to that document's section 4.


=============================================================================
1. WHAT THIS IS
=============================================================================

One paragraph, for the App Store listing and for keeping yourself honest:

  An infinite canvas built for handwriting, with an AI tutor living inside the
  canvas rather than in a chat window beside it. You bring problems in — the
  app generates them, or you import a page and mark them — you work them by
  hand with a pencil, and the tutor watches your strokes as you write. When you
  stall, erase in frustration, or take a wrong turn, it places a hint on the
  canvas next to your work. It never leads with the answer. You bring your own
  API key; nothing you write leaves your device except the problem region you
  are actively working on, sent to the provider you chose.

The single sentence that decides arguments:

  We are building the thing that notices the moment your reasoning bends.
  Everything else is in service of that.

WHAT THIS IS NOT
  - Not a homework-answer app. See section 3.
  - Not a note-taking app. Notability, GoodNotes, and Freeform own that. If a
    feature only makes sense for note-taking and not for working problems, it
    is out of v1 and probably out of the product.
  - Not a curriculum. We do not own content, standards alignment, or grading.
  - Not a chat interface with a canvas attached. If a feature works equally
    well as a chat message, it is not this product.


=============================================================================
2. WHO IT IS FOR
=============================================================================

v1 target user, precisely:

  A high-school-through-early-undergraduate student working symbolic math or
  quantitative science by hand on an iPad with an Apple Pencil, on practice
  sets and homework, alone, at a desk, with intent to actually learn it.

Design consequences of that specificity:
  - Sessions are 20-90 minutes, not 3 minutes. Startup cost can be higher than
    a note app; interruption cost is much higher.
  - The user's hands are on the Pencil. Every interaction the tutor requires
    should be reachable without putting it down.
  - The user is frequently stuck and mildly demoralized. Tone is a feature.
  - The user is not paying for a subscription in v1 — they are pasting an API
    key. That selects for a technically comfortable early adopter, which is
    fine and correct for v1, and is a distribution ceiling to solve later.

Explicitly NOT the v1 user: essay writers, note-takers, collaborative study
groups, teachers assigning work, and anyone on a device without a stylus.


=============================================================================
3. THE NON-NEGOTIABLE: HINTS, NEVER ANSWERS
=============================================================================

This is a product constraint, not a setting, not a config value, and not
something a user preference toggle may override.

Two independent reasons, both sufficient on their own:

  PEDAGOGICAL. The value in the prototype was never the answer. It was
  identifying the class of error mid-work. A student who receives the answer
  learns that they were wrong; a student who receives the smallest sufficient
  nudge learns why, and produces the step themselves. The second is the
  product.

  COMMERCIAL AND INSTITUTIONAL. An app positioned as "AI that does your
  homework" has a short life: app-store scrutiny, school-district blocks,
  academic-integrity press, and a permanent ceiling on institutional sales. An
  app positioned as "AI that catches you the moment you go wrong" is sellable
  to the institution that would otherwise ban it. This is the difference
  between a feature and a company.

Hard rules, enforced in Layer 1 and covered by tests:

  R1. The tutor never emits a final answer to the problem as stated, at any
      hint level, under any prompt, including when the student explicitly asks
      for it.
  R2. The tutor never executes more than one forward step in a single hint.
  R3. Hint level never advances without an intervening student attempt or an
      explicit student request for more help.
  R4. The tutor refuses to operate when the app is in Exam Mode, and refuses
      when the student states they are in a proctored assessment. Refusal is a
      first-class response type, not an error.
  R5. Response parsing rejects and regenerates any provider output that
      violates R1 or R2. The model is not trusted to obey the ladder; the
      ladder is enforced by our code on the way out.

  OPINION: R5 is the one people skip and the one that matters. Prompting alone
  will hold ~95% of the time, and the 5% is exactly the screenshot that ends up
  on a teacher's desk. The enforcement layer is a Layer 1 validator that runs on
  every parsed response and can force a retry at the same level. Building it in
  M6 costs maybe a day. Retrofitting it after a bad review costs the launch.


=============================================================================
4. THE FOUR-LEVEL HINT LADDER
=============================================================================

Worked against the prototype's real example throughout:
    simplify (3x^2 - 12)/(x^2 + x - 6)
    student has written: cancelled x^2 against x^2, then 12 against 6

  LEVEL 1 — ORIENT
    Points attention at the location of the trouble. Names no mathematics.
    Answers "where should I be looking?"
    Example: "Take another look at the step where you cancelled. Something
              there isn't allowed yet."
    Length: one sentence. Never contains an operator or a variable.

  LEVEL 2 — PRINCIPLE
    States the general rule that was violated, in the abstract. Does not touch
    the student's numbers. Answers "what rule am I breaking?"
    Example: "You can only cancel factors, not terms. A sum has to be factored
              before anything in it can cancel."
    Length: one or two sentences. May contain generic notation, never the
    student's specific expression.

  LEVEL 3 — APPLIED NUDGE
    Applies the principle to the student's actual expression and poses the next
    sub-question. Stops immediately before executing it. Answers "what do I do
    next?"
    Example: "Factor the top and bottom completely before cancelling anything.
              Start with the top — what's the common factor? Then: what pair
              multiplies to -6 and adds to +1?"
    Length: up to three sentences. Ends on a question the student answers.

  LEVEL 4 — ONE WORKED STEP
    Performs exactly one step and hands control back. Never the last step of
    the problem. Answers "I'm still stuck, show me one."
    Example: "The top factors to 3(x^2 - 4). Take it from there — x^2 - 4 has
              one more factorization in it."
    Requires: an explicit student request AND at least two prior attempts on
    this problem. Never fires from a stall alone.
    Hard stop: if the only remaining step is the final one, Level 4 degrades to
    Level 3 rather than finishing the problem.

ESCALATION RULES
  - Every problem starts at Level 1.
  - Advance one level only when: the student has produced new strokes since the
    last hint AND the error persists, or the student explicitly asks for more.
  - Never skip a level.
  - Never descend a level within a problem.
  - Level 4 is capped at twice per problem. After the second, the tutor offers
    to set the problem aside and return to it, rather than continuing.
  - If the student's new strokes show the error was corrected, reset to Level 1
    and say so briefly. Recognition is a hint type.

  OPINION: the "reset and acknowledge" behavior is worth building even though it
  produces no instructional content. In the prototype the single most motivating
  message was "Good — numerator is right so far." The tutor being visibly aware
  that you fixed it is a large part of why the loop feels like a person.


=============================================================================
5. THE TUTOR LOOP
=============================================================================

The prototype polled on a fixed interval. That is the wrong architecture and
replacing it is the core technical bet of this product. The tutor fires on
events, not on a timer.

TRIGGER EVENTS (any one arms an evaluation)

  T1. STALL — no new stroke for `stallThreshold` while a problem is active and
      has at least one stroke of student work. The primary trigger.
  T2. ERASE BURST — more than `eraseBurstFraction` of the strokes written in
      the last `eraseBurstWindow` have been erased. Signals the student has
      detected an error but not diagnosed it. Highest-value moment in the whole
      session; the student is receptive and specifically confused.
  T3. EXPLICIT ASK — student writes "?" inside a problem region, or taps the
      hint affordance. Bypasses cooldown. Never suppressed.
  T4. LONG DWELL — more than `dwellThreshold` on one problem with continuing
      strokes but no structural progress. Catches the student who is busily
      going nowhere; T1 never fires for them.
  T5. PROBLEM COMPLETE — student's strokes enter the next problem's region, or
      a boxed/underlined result is detected. Triggers evaluation, not a hint:
      records whether the problem was solved and how, for the session summary.

SUPPRESSION RULES (all evaluated before a hint is placed)
  - Never fire while a stroke is in progress.
  - `hintCooldown` since the last placed hint, except for T3.
  - `postHintQuietPeriod` after a hint is placed, during which T1 cannot fire
    at all — the student is reading.
  - `maxHintsPerProblem` cap, after which the tutor goes quiet until T3.
  - Never fire on a problem region with no student strokes in it. An empty
    problem is not a stuck student; it is a student who hasn't started.

THE EVALUATION PIPELINE (Layer 1 orchestrates; Layer 2 does the I/O)

  1. Trigger fires. Tutor state machine decides evaluate or suppress.
  2. Assemble context for the ACTIVE PROBLEM ONLY:
       - a rasterized crop of the problem region (Layer 2 protocol, see below)
       - the problem statement as text, if known
       - stroke-order and timing metadata as structured text
       - hints already given on this problem, and the current ladder level
       - the student's prior error classes this session
  3. Send through the AIProvider protocol.
  4. Parse into a typed TutorResponse: { level, hintText, targetAnchor,
     errorClass, confidence, refusal? }.
  5. Validate against R1/R2. On violation, regenerate once at the same level;
     on second violation, drop to a level-1 orienting hint and log it.
  6. Below `confidenceFloor`, place nothing. Silence beats a wrong hint —
     a hint pointing at a correct step actively destroys trust.
  7. Place the hint on the canvas, anchored near `targetAnchor`, never
     overlapping student ink.

  OPINION — SEND AN IMAGE, NOT A POINT LIST. Frontier models read a rendered
  crop of handwritten math far more reliably than any serialization of stroke
  coordinates, and the prototype proved the image path works. Stroke data is
  still captured and stored (Invariant 2 requires it) and is sent alongside as
  *metadata* — order, timing, pauses, erasures — which is signal the image
  cannot carry. This creates a Layer 2 protocol, `StrokeRasterizing`, because
  Layer 1 cannot rasterize anything without CoreGraphics. Layer 1 decides WHAT
  region to render and at what scale; Layer 2 renders it. Rendering parameters
  are config values, because image resolution is the single biggest lever on
  both cost and accuracy and you will tune it constantly.

HINT PRESENTATION
  Hints appear as canvas objects — not a sidebar, not a toast, not a modal.
  They are pinned near the relevant work, they persist, they are dismissible,
  and they scroll with the canvas. A hint the student scrolled away from is a
  hint they can scroll back to.


=============================================================================
6. v1 SCOPE
=============================================================================

The test for inclusion: does removing it break the loop in section 5?

IN — v1 SHIPS WITH
  - Infinite canvas: pan, zoom, effectively unbounded extent.
  - Ink: pencil, highlighter, eraser. Color and width selection. Undo/redo.
  - Normalized stroke model as source of truth (Invariant 2), with a cached
    render representation.
  - Palm rejection and Pencil-only-draw mode.
  - Multiple documents, local persistence through the repository protocol,
    mutations as discrete ordered operations (Invariant 4).
  - Work regions: first-class model objects with bounds, a kind (v1 ships one,
    `.mathProblem`), state (untouched / in-progress / complete / set-aside),
    hint history, and level. Named `WorkRegion`, not `ProblemRegion` — see
    section 10, C2.
  - Problem creation two ways: AI-generated worksheets, and manual region
    marking over an imported page.
  - Import a PDF page or photo as a canvas backdrop, over which the student
    marks problem regions by hand.
  - BYO API key in Keychain, behind a protocol. One provider adapter shipped
    (Anthropic), with a second stubbed to prove the seam is real.
  - The full tutor loop and four-level ladder.
  - Exam Mode: an explicit switch that disables the tutor entirely.
  - End-of-session summary: problems attempted, error classes, hints used.
  - Settings: the config object exposed as a tuning screen, behind a
    developer-mode flag.

OUT — DEFERRED, WITH REASONS
  - Automatic problem segmentation from an imported page. This is a real
    computer-vision and layout problem and it is the single biggest schedule
    risk in the product. Manual region marking gets 90% of the value for
    roughly 5% of the effort, and gives us labelled data for building the
    automatic version later. v1.1.

    OPINION: I know this is closest to your original framing — "import
    homework, projects, books." I am not cutting import. I am cutting
    *automatic* problem detection. The student imports the page and taps four
    times to mark four problems. If you disagree, the counter-argument is that
    manual marking is friction at the exact moment of highest abandonment risk
    — first use. That is a real argument and I'd want to see it fail with a
    real user before rebuilding the milestone plan around it.

  - Handwriting-to-LaTeX / symbolic recognition on device. Not needed; the
    model reads the image.
  - Shapes, multiple fonts, sticky notes, and the rest of Freeform parity.
    None of it serves the loop.
  - Sync and CloudKit. Invariant 4 keeps the seam open; v1 does not use it.
  - Cross-session student modeling. The summary is per-session in v1. Persisting
    an error profile across sessions is the strongest retention feature in the
    product and it deserves its own design pass, not a bolt-on.
  - Accounts, auth, subscriptions, collaboration, teacher dashboards.
  - iPhone, Mac, Android, Windows. Per existing decisions.


=============================================================================
7. CONFIGURATION OBJECT — TutorConfig
=============================================================================

Everything below lives in one named struct in Layer 1, with documented
defaults. No literals scattered in call sites.

  stallThreshold             4.0 s     T1 fires after this much stroke silence.
                                       Lower = pushier tutor. Below ~2.5s it
                                       interrupts mid-thought.
  hintCooldown              20.0 s     Minimum spacing between placed hints.
  postHintQuietPeriod        8.0 s     T1 fully disabled after a hint lands.
  dwellThreshold            90.0 s     T4 threshold for busy-but-stuck.
  eraseBurstFraction         0.40      Fraction of recent strokes erased to
                                       trigger T2.
  eraseBurstWindow          10.0 s     Window over which that fraction counts.
  maxHintsPerProblem         6         Cap before the tutor goes quiet.
  maxLevel4PerProblem        2         Cap on worked steps.
  confidenceFloor            0.60      Below this, place nothing.
  regionRenderScale          2.0       Raster scale for the problem crop.
  regionRenderMaxEdge     1536 px      Long-edge cap on the sent image. The
                                       main cost/accuracy lever.
  regionPaddingFraction      0.08      Padding around the region when cropping.
  strokeDebounce           400 ms      Stroke-completion debounce. Named for
                                       the PencilKit partial-stroke behavior.
  ladderRequiresAttempt      true      Feature flag. Off = escalate on stall
                                       alone. For testing only.
  enforceNoAnswer            true      Feature flag on the R1/R2 validator.
                                       Never ship this false.

8. MILESTONES
=============================================================================

Each ends with something running on a physical iPad, and states what you should
see when it works.

  M0 — SKELETON
    Xcode project, three targets matching the three layers, Layer 1 as a
    standalone Swift package with a Linux CI job running its tests.
    You should see: app launches to a blank canvas; CI green on Linux.

  M1 — INK
    PencilKit capture -> normalized stroke model -> render. Infinite pan/zoom.
    Undo/redo. Palm rejection.
    You should see: you can draw, and you cannot tell it apart from Freeform by
    feel. If you can, stop and fix it before M2. Ink feel outranks everything.

  M2 — PERSISTENCE
    Documents through the repository protocol. Mutations as ordered operations.
    UUID + lastModified + owner on every model object.
    You should see: draw, force-quit, relaunch, everything is exactly there.

  M3 — PROBLEM MODEL
    Problem regions, per-problem state, manual region creation by lasso or tap.
    You should see: circle an area, it becomes "Problem 1", its state chip
    changes as you write in it.

  M4 — PROVIDER SEAM
    Keychain key entry, AIProvider protocol, Anthropic adapter, region
    rasterization. A debug button that sends the active region and shows the
    raw model reading of your handwriting.
    You should see: write something messy, tap debug, watch the model read it
    back correctly. This is the go/no-go for the whole product.

  M5 — TUTOR LOOP, ONE LEVEL
    Stall detection (T1), evaluation pipeline, hints placed on canvas at
    Level 2 only.
    You should see: write a wrong step, stop for four seconds, a hint appears
    next to your work without you doing anything.

  M6 — THE LADDER
    All four levels, escalation rules, all triggers T2-T5, cooldowns,
    suppression, the R1/R2 validator, TutorConfig wired to a settings screen.
    You should see: hints escalate only after you try again; asking for the
    answer outright gets you a nudge instead.

  M7 — PROBLEMS IN
    AI worksheet generation into problem regions. PDF/photo import as backdrop
    with manual region marking.
    You should see: "generate 10 ALEKS-style problems", they lay out on the
    canvas with working room, and the tutor works against them.

  M8 — SESSION SUMMARY, EXAM MODE, TESTFLIGHT
    End-of-session error-pattern summary. Exam Mode switch. Onboarding for key
    entry. Ship to TestFlight.
    You should see: finish a session, get a summary that tells you something
    about yourself you didn't already know.

  OPINION ON ORDERING: M4 before M5 is deliberate. The riskiest assumption in
  the product is that a frontier model reads *your* handwriting, at *your*
  raster settings, reliably enough to diagnose from. The prototype proved it for
  a Freeform screenshot on a Mac. It has not been proven for our render path.
  Find out in M4, when the sunk cost is four milestones instead of eight.


=============================================================================
9. OPEN QUESTIONS
=============================================================================

  Q1. Cost per hint. At the default raster settings, unknown. Measure in M4 and
      set regionRenderMaxEdge from real numbers. If a 60-minute session costs
      more than a dollar of the student's own key, the defaults are wrong.
  Q2. Latency budget. How long between stall and hint before the loop stops
      feeling live? My guess is 3-4 seconds of model time is tolerable because
      the student is already stopped. Untested.
  Q3. Conflict semantics. Ordered operations are chosen to keep a CRDT possible.
      We are explicitly deferring real merge semantics; note the deferral in the
      decisions log at M2 rather than pretending it's solved.
  Q4. Error-class taxonomy. The session summary needs a fixed vocabulary of
      error classes for the model to select from, or the summaries will be
      mush. Needs a design pass before M8. Partially resolved: the taxonomy is
      namespaced by domain from the start — see section 10, C5.
  Q5. What happens on a wrong hint. There is no correction path in this brief.
      A "that's not my problem" gesture that re-evaluates is probably necessary
      and is not scoped.

10. FORWARD COMPATIBILITY WITH v1.5 — NOT OPTIONAL
=============================================================================

Added 2026-08-13 after v1.5 was scoped to include text-based work: literature
analysis, document analysis, reading assignments, writing, and mechanics. Full
detail in brief/v15-addendum.txt.

Six changes to v1. None of them add a v1 feature. All of them exist purely to
prevent v1.5 from being a schema migration against real user data — the exact
failure Invariant 4 is written to prevent. Two of them (C2, C5) are pure
naming and cost nothing today, which is why they are the ones most likely to
get skipped.

  C1  [M2]  Canvas elements are polymorphic. The document is a collection of
            `CanvasElement`, not a collection of strokes. v1 kinds:
            `.strokeGroup`, `.image`, `.hint`. v1.5 adds `.textBlock`,
            `.sourceDocument`, `.outlineNode`. UUID + lastModified + owner on
            every element regardless of kind.

  C2  [M3]  `WorkRegion`, not `ProblemRegion`, carrying a `kind`. v1 ships
            `.mathProblem` only. v1.5 adds `.readingPassage`,
            `.outlineSection`, `.draftSection`. State value "solved" is named
            "complete" for the same reason.

  C3  [M6]  `HintLadder` is a protocol declaring the four levels, escalation
            rules, and validation hooks. `MathLadder` conforms in v1;
            `WritingLadder` and `ReadingLadder` conform in v1.5 without the
            tutor state machine changing. Invariant 3 applied to our own
            domain logic, not just to external dependencies.

  C4  [M5]  `EvaluationContext` is composable, not "an image plus metadata."
            v1 uses `.rasterizedRegion`. v1.5 adds `.textSpan`,
            `.documentChunks`, `.outlineGraph` without the section 5 pipeline
            changing shape.

  C5  [M8]  Error classes are namespaced by domain from the start:
            `math.cancellation.termsNotFactors`,
            `writing.paragraph.claimDrift`, `reading.inference.unsupported`.
            Flat taxonomies do not survive a second domain.

  C6  [M7]  Imported documents store extracted text, with character offsets
            and page mapping, even though v1 never reads it. v1 renders the
            backdrop image; v1.5 needs the text. Extraction at import time is
            nearly free. Backfilling it across every document every user has
            already created is a migration written under pressure.

The v1 hard rules in section 3 gain R6 in v1.5 — the prose rule — which is
stricter than R1/R2 because in writing the prose IS the artifact, and a leaked
paragraph is a submittable deliverable rather than a partial hint. R6 does not
constrain v1, but the R1/R2 validator built in M6 must be structured so a
second domain's rule set drops in beside it.
